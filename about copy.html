NSU: a brief history

The first private university in Bangladesh

Est. 1992 About NSU

North South University (NSU)

North South University, the first private university in Bangladesh, was established in 1992 by the Foundation for Promotion of Education and Research (FPER) – a non‑profit, non‑commercial, non‑political organization. The FPER later became the NSU Foundation and is now called The North South Foundation for Education and Research.

The Foundation brought together eminent industrialists, patrons of education, philanthropists, academics and senior civil servants. In the early 1990s, they set out to build a world‑class university as a centre of excellence in the private sector.

⚖️ Legislative foundation

At that time there was no law to establish a private university. The founders took the burden upon themselves and assisted the government in formulating and enacting the first Private University Act (PUA‑1992).

Pursuant to the new act, NSU was approved. The University was formally inaugurated on 10 February 1993 and started its journey modestly.

In 2012, following PUA‑2010, the NSU Foundation formed the NSU Trust with the same group, vesting management in the Board of Trustees (BOT).

🏛️ Governance structure

The Honorable President of Bangladesh is the Chancellor of NSU.

The Syndicate, headed by the Vice‑Chancellor, is next to the BOT. The Vice‑Chancellor is the CEO, managing academic and administrative affairs according to PUA‑2010 and the BOT’s policies.

A Pro Vice‑Chancellor and a Treasurer assist him. All are appointed by the Chancellor on the BOT’s recommendation.

📚 Academic excellence

NSU is modelled on US universities – semester system, credit hours, letter grades. Early undergraduate curricula (Economics, Business, Computer Science) were largely adapted from the University of Illinois at Urbana‑Champaign, approved by the UGC.

An International Advisory Board of scholars from around the world counsels on academic matters. NSU delivers a robust general education curriculum, has a strategic plan, and implements instructional learning assessment for all degree programs, moving toward institutional accreditation.
